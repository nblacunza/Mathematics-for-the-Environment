Chapter 1

Solving nonlinear equations

Roots, iteration, and numerical intuition.

Open chapter →
Dichotomy, secant, and Newton methods are introduced as practical tools to solve nonlinear equations in one and several dimensions.
Chapter 2

Elementary topology

Open sets, compactness, and structure.

A gentle entry into topological language, including openness, closedness, compactness, continuity, and the geometric intuition behind these concepts.
Chapter 3

Linear algebra and applications

Vectors, matrices, and geometric action.

Core notions of vector spaces, matrices, norms, eigendirections, and coordinate systems, always connected to environmental modelling examples.
Chapter 4

Solving linear systems Ax = b

Direct and iterative solution strategies.

Gaussian elimination, conditioning, and iterative schemes are presented to understand how linear systems arise and are solved in scientific practice.
Chapter 5

Computing eigenvalues and eigenvectors

Spectra, modes, and dominant directions.

Power methods, spectral ideas, and modal viewpoints help reveal the hidden structure of linear operators and dynamical systems.
Chapter 6

Interpolation and approximation

Building functions from data and samples.

Polynomial and piecewise approximation, interpolation nodes, and approximation error are framed as bridges between discrete data and continuous fields.
Chapter 7

Numerical integration methods

Approximating areas, fluxes, and totals.

Quadrature rules are introduced to estimate integrals accurately, with attention to convergence, stability, and practical implementation.
Chapter 8

Introduction to ODEs

Flows, trajectories, and time evolution.

Ordinary differential equations are introduced as models of temporal change, linking equations, vector fields, and solution curves.
Chapter 9

Linear autonomous systems

Phase portraits near equilibrium.

Stability, invariant directions, and matrix exponentials provide the first full dynamical picture of autonomous systems.
Chapter 10

Numerically solving ODEs

Discretising dynamics with care.

Euler, Runge–Kutta, and related methods are presented with a focus on accuracy, stability, and what numerical trajectories really mean.
Chapter 11

Elements of topological dynamics

Recurrence, invariance, and asymptotics.

The text moves toward deeper dynamical ideas such as attractors, recurrence, invariant sets, and the topological view of long-term behaviour.
Chapter 12

Basic facts of the climate sciences

The physical backdrop of the climate system.

Fundamental processes, balances, and scales in the climate system are introduced to connect mathematics with real Earth-system behaviour.
Chapter 13

Nonlinear systems and bifurcations

How qualitative change emerges.

Fixed points, cycles, thresholds, and bifurcations are explored to show how simple systems can reorganise into radically different regimes.
Chapter 14

Studying the atmosphere and oceans

Dynamics of the fluid Earth.

Atmospheric and oceanic motions are studied through equations, balances, waves, and circulation patterns central to geophysical fluid dynamics.
Chapter 15

Applications to ecology

Dynamics in living systems.

Mathematical ideas are transferred to ecological systems, highlighting interaction networks, population dynamics, and environmental feedbacks.
Chapter 16

Numerical bifurcation analysis of climate models

Computing regime shifts in practice.

Continuation methods and computational diagnostics are used to detect tipping points, hysteresis, and multi-stability in climate models.
Chapter 17

Natural variability and forced changes

Signals, fluctuations, and long-term change.

The final stage contrasts intrinsic variability with externally forced response, tying together dynamics, data, and interpretation.
